## High-Conviction Trends

### 1. Nvidia's crisis pushes customers toward custom chips
- **Trigger:** the Reuters report on the SEC accounting probe ([20 Nov](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/exclusiveus-secs-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-5042460)), the California subpoena (19 Nov) and the Commerce licensing rule (16 Nov).
- **In plain English:** the world's main AI chip company is under investigation and has no permanent boss. Its customers don't want to depend on one supplier with legal trouble. Many of them already design their own AI chips with help from Broadcom, so every step away from Nvidia tends to mean more work for Broadcom.
- **Second-order chain:** Nvidia's credibility falls -> big cloud firms speed up their in-house chips -> custom-chip designers (Broadcom) and the factories that make every chip (TSMC) keep getting paid, whoever wins.
- **Horizon:** months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a quick, narrow resolution of the probe plus a credible new CEO, or the cloud firms cutting all AI spending at once.

### 2. Cheaper copper helps the companies that build the grid
- **Trigger:** copper's bear market ([Reuters, 23 Nov](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/lme-copper-briefly-falls-below-12000-a-ton-as-tech-selloff-deepens-5044280)), while ABB's $2.65bn purchase of Ziehl-Abegg (fans and cooling, 23 Nov) shows electrification spending carrying on.
- **In plain English:** copper fell because investors sold, not because fewer cables and transformers are being ordered. Companies that make electrical equipment buy copper as a raw material, so their costs fall while their order books stay full.
- **Second-order chain:** copper falls -> raw-material costs fall for switchgear, cable and transformer makers -> their margins widen on long order books -> Schneider Electric and Siemens Energy (already on the watchlist) benefit.
- **Horizon:** months to years.
- **What would invalidate it:** big cloud firms cutting data-centre spending, which is about 30% of Schneider's orders.

### 3. Rising rates push money into defensive, pound-based businesses
- **Trigger:** about 93.5% odds of a December US rate rise, and the VIX at 35.9 ([Reuters, 23 Nov](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wall-street-futures-jump-after-major-indexes-enter-correction-territory-5044226)).
- **In plain English:** when fear rises, big funds sell tech and buy businesses whose profits don't depend on the economy. Regulated utilities like National Grid earn an allowed return on their networks whatever happens to AI. National Grid rose 1.07% on a day the US fell 3.7%.
- **Second-order chain:** tech sell-off -> funds rotate -> regulated utilities and quality drug makers hold up -> they cushion a portfolio that is too concentrated in AI.
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a soft US inflation reading on Wednesday that removes the rate rise and sends money rushing back into tech.

### 4. Europe's legal and energy deals (Bayer, Eni)
- **Trigger:** Bayer's Roundup settlement approval (reported 23 Nov, single source) and Vår Energi's $4.4bn North Sea deal (23 Nov).
- **In plain English:** a court capping Bayer's weedkiller lawsuits removes the main thing holding its shares down. Eni's majority-owned Vår Energi has locked in high gas prices (about $110 per barrel of oil equivalent on 80% of the added gas) for the next year.
- **Second-order chain:** the legal cap lowers Bayer's borrowing risk, freeing cash for drug research. North Sea assets move from US oil majors to European producers, which affects oil service contractors.
- **Horizon:** months.
- **What would invalidate it:** appeals reopening Bayer's liability; OPEC+ (29 Nov) or an Iran ceasefire pushing gas prices sharply lower.
