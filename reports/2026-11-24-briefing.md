### 1. Huang's return revives the AI trade - but the politics are now part of the price
- **Trigger:** Nvidia board reinstates Huang, 23 Nov ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-stock-jensen-huang-ai-trade.html); [Yahoo live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/live/nvidia-live-updates-jensen-huang-reinstated-as-ceo-trump-says-he-convinced-board-165423793.html)).
- **In plain English:** The founder whose exit crashed Nvidia's shares is back, so investors who had dumped AI shares rushed back in. Chip, memory and server suppliers rose most. But the investigations into chips leaking to China and how Nvidia reported its Asian sales have not gone away - they have become more political because the President says he pushed for Huang's return.
- **Second-order chain:** Nvidia stable -> lenders and investors more willing to fund AI data centres again -> builders of power, grid and cooling kit (Siemens, Siemens Energy, Schneider) get a steadier order outlook; copper gets a reprieve. Separately, new US licence rules for chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil may shift AI-server demand toward US, European and Middle East sites (inference).
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** SEC/DOJ action naming Nvidia, Treasury rejecting its explanation, or executive resignations.

### 2. US rates are going up, not down - and Wednesday's inflation number matters
- **Trigger:** futures price about a 92% chance of a Fed **rate rise** on 9 Dec; US 10-year yield about 4.92-4.94%; dollar near a 19-month high vs the euro (Reuters via Investing.com, 23-24 Nov). US PCE inflation due Wed 25 Nov.
- **In plain English:** US inflation (3.7% in September on the PCE measure) is well above the 2% target, so the Fed is expected to raise rates. Higher rates make borrowing dearer, weigh on expensive growth shares and on metals like copper, and help banks earn more on the gap between loan and deposit rates.
- **Second-order chain:** higher US rates -> strong dollar -> pressure on copper (your COPG) -> but a tailwind for banks with big dollar deposits such as HSBC (Hong Kong's currency is pegged to the dollar, so its rates follow US rates). Consumers feel the squeeze: UK retailer AO World warned of softer trading today, citing rising rates.
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a soft PCE figure or Fed officials pushing back on a hike.

### 3. Big pharma is paying up for growth before its patents expire
- **Trigger:** Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for $175 a share in cash, about $41bn ([Sanofi press release, 24 Nov](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/24/3409002/0/en/press-release-sanofi-to-acquire-insmed-in-strategic-step-that-strengthens-long-term-future.html); [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/sanofi-strikes-41-billion-deal-to-acquire-us-drugmaker-insmed-5047163)).
- **In plain English:** Sanofi's best-selling drug, Dupixent, will eventually lose patent protection, so Sanofi is buying a company with a fast-growing lung-disease drug (Brinsupri) to keep sales growing. Buyers' shares often fall on deal day because investors dislike the price and the extra debt - which can create a short-term opportunity in a sound company.
- **Second-order chain:** patent cliffs -> big pharma buys growth with debt -> mid-sized drug companies with approved, growing medicines get a "takeover floor" under their value; companies that already have growth (like your Lilly) look more valuable.
- **Horizon:** days to weeks for Sanofi's reaction; months for the M&A theme.
- **What would invalidate it:** antitrust problems, or Brinsupri growth slowing further (Q3 +29% quarter on quarter, down from 44-49%).

### 4. Europe may have to supply more of its own air defence
- **Trigger:** FT reports the Pentagon is delaying about 60 Patriot interceptor missiles for Ukraine (via Investing.com, 24 Nov - **single source**).
- **In plain English:** If US missiles arrive later or not at all, European governments need to buy more from European makers. BAE owns part of MBDA, which makes Europe's competing Aster missile system, and has a wider defence order book.
- **Horizon:** months to years.
- **What would invalidate it:** a Ukraine ceasefire, or the US approving the shipment quickly.

### 5. Cheap oil despite the Hormuz disruption
- **Trigger:** Brent about $89.10, lowest since 27 Aug, on rising supply and a 52m-barrel October stockpile build (Reuters via Investing.com, 24 Nov 07:00 GMT), even as three tankers hit mines near Hormuz.
- **In plain English:** Oil is getting cheaper because more is coming from elsewhere. Jet fuel is one of an airline's biggest costs, so airlines benefit - with a delay, because they buy fuel in advance at fixed prices. The risk is that Hormuz escalates and oil spikes.
- **Horizon:** weeks.
- **What would invalidate it:** Brent back above about $97, or OPEC+ cuts.
