## High-Conviction Trends

### 1. Inflation is back, so rates go up: good for oil producers and banks, bad for expensive growth shares
- **Trigger:** US October PCE 3.8%, core 3.3% (25 Nov); 94.1% chance of a Fed rise on 8-9 Dec (CME FedWatch via Investing.com); Bank of Japan board member Tamura said rates must reach "neutral" soon and a 0.5-point rise may be needed (Investing.com, 26 Nov).
- **In plain English:** Prices in the US are rising faster again, partly because the war around Iran keeps oil expensive. To cool them, the US central bank is expected to raise interest rates. That makes borrowing dearer and makes the far-off profits of growth companies worth less today, which is why tech shares fell. The same forces help companies that sell oil (they earn more when prices are high) and banks (they earn more on the gap between what they pay savers and charge borrowers). Japan is raising rates for the first time in decades, which helps Japanese banks in particular.
- **Second-order chain:** Hormuz disruption -> oil about $95 -> inflation stays high -> rate rises -> growth shares fall, while oil majors (Shell) and banks (SMFG, HSBC) gain -> but a stronger dollar pushes copper down, which hurts COPG.
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a weak US jobs report in early December, or a ceasefire that reopens Hormuz and drops oil.

### 2. The AI sell-off is catching companies whose own orders are fine
- **Trigger:** Nvidia's governance crisis and the Nasdaq 100's intraday 20% drawdown (25 Nov); Treasury deadline passed with no public board answer; Siemens Energy and Infineon down with no company news.
- **In plain English:** Investors are selling anything linked to AI because of doubts about Nvidia's leadership and the investigations. That includes companies that make power equipment, power chips and copper. Several have record order books (Siemens Energy reports a €164bn backlog). If their orders keep turning into sales, their share prices may have fallen further than their business justifies. But nobody knows when the selling stops, and next week's hearings could make it worse.
- **Second-order chain:** Nvidia doubts -> everything AI-linked is sold -> grid-equipment and power-chip makers fall with no news of their own -> possible bargains (Siemens Energy, Infineon) once the hearings pass. **Kioxia's 12.6% jump shows companies with concrete AI news are the first to recover.**
- **Horizon:** days to weeks for the selling; months for the order books.
- **What would invalidate it:** the hearings reveal real export violations, an SEC case is confirmed, or a big cloud company cuts its spending.

### 3. US "excess capacity" tariffs: China hit hard, allies mostly spared
- **Trigger:** on 25 Nov the US Trade Representative proposed country-by-country tariffs on 14 economies under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act. China faces an extra 20% (40% if it breaks the trade truce). The EU, Japan, Korea and Taiwan stay inside their agreed 15% caps. Brazilian aircraft would face 10%. These are **proposals only**: January 2027 at the earliest, with hearings and legal challenges expected (Korea Times, 26 Nov; CBS; law-firm notes). China said it "reserves the right to take necessary measures".
- **In plain English:** The US wants extra import taxes on goods from countries it says overproduce. For Europe and Japan the extra cost looks small, so the sell-off in German shares like Siemens on Wednesday may have been too much. China is the big loser. The tariffs also add a little to US inflation, which feeds Trend 1.
- **Second-order chain:** tariffs -> higher US prices -> more pressure on the Fed -> stronger dollar. European exporters were sold on the headline even though their rate looks capped.
- **Horizon:** months.
- **What would invalidate it:** courts or Congress block the tariffs, or the EU/Japan caps are broken.

### 4. Europe caps hybrid car imports from 1 Dec
- **Trigger:** European Commission provisional quotas on hybrid and plug-in hybrid imports from all origins, effective 1 Dec, for 200 days before a member-state vote (Reuters via Investing.com, 25 Nov).
- **In plain English:** Europe is limiting how many cheaper hybrid cars, mostly Chinese, can come in. That protects European carmakers' home sales, which is why their shares jumped. But Chinese firms can get round the cap by building cars inside Europe. That would mean new factories, and new factories need automation kit from firms like Siemens (which you own).
- **Why no car shares today:** BMW and Volkswagen revenue is shrinking (-5.8% and -6.3%). The rules are provisional and could be voted down. Chasing a 5-6% one-day jump in shrinking businesses is poor risk/reward. See Rejected.
- **Horizon:** months to years.

### 5. The Hormuz shock reaches UK households
- **Trigger:** typical UK energy bills to rise 26% from January, from £1,723 to £2,166 a year (BBC, 25 Nov; Ofgem's own release not seen). The Bank of England's Clare Lombardelli warned food inflation could reach 5% next year (Guardian, 25 Nov). Poundland entered administration, putting about 10,000 jobs and around 600 stores at risk (BBC; Guardian, 25 Nov).
- **In plain English:** British households will have about £440 a year less for everything else. Weaker shops fail and stronger discounters pick up their customers. But we checked Tesco, Sainsbury's and B&M and none passed. Poundland is too small to move Tesco's profits, Sainsbury's margin is 0.2%, and B&M has already doubled this year.
- **Horizon:** months.
