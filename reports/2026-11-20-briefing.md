## High-Conviction Trends

### 1. The AI share sell-off spreads worldwide, even as Anthropic lists
- **Trigger:** Anthropic +17% on day one, but the S&P 500 -2.02%, Taiwan -6.2% and the Nikkei -4.77% (19-20 Nov; Reuters via [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/asian-shares-head-for-worst-week-since-2020-as-ai-trade-gloom-fails-to-abate-5040496), [Morning Bid](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/morning-bid-anthropics-splashy-ipo-fails-to-lift-ai-boat-5040706)).
- **In plain English:** the largest AI company yet to list did well on its first day. But many investors used the moment to sell other AI shares rather than buy more. Reuters points to Nvidia's leadership turmoil, the AMD short report, political pushback against data centres and worries about AI debt. Companies whose recent growth depends on AI spending, such as Asian chipmakers, cable makers and data-centre landlords, were hit hardest. Your VUAG, Nvidia and IBM are all exposed to this.
- **Second-order chain:** AI shares fall. Lenders get more cautious about financing data centres (CoreWeave's 2031 bonds already yield above 16%, a level that signals doubt about repayment). Fewer data-centre builds then mean fewer orders for cables, materials and cooling. Suppliers whose demand comes from power grids rather than data centres should hold up better (Trend 3).
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** Nvidia and Anthropic stabilise and Fed rate-rise pricing eases, which would make this a short burst of forced selling that reverses.

### 2. A strong dollar and a likely Fed rise force selling of metals
- **Trigger:** CME FedWatch shows 97.3% odds of a 0.25-point US rate rise in December. The dollar index (the dollar against a basket of major currencies) is about 103.7. Gold is at a 14-month low and copper is at $12,190.68/t (20 Nov; [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/gold-slides-to-14mth-low-set-for-weekly-drop-amid-fed-hike-bets-5040648)).
- **In plain English:** a US rate rise makes the dollar stronger, and metals priced in dollars become dearer for everyone else, so demand and prices fall. Investors losing money on tech also sell what is easy to sell, such as gold, to raise cash. Mining shares fall further than the metals because their profits shrink faster. That is why COPG is down 29.9% in three months.
- **Second-order chain:** metal prices fall. Miners delay new mines, which tightens future supply and supports the long-run price. Meanwhile companies that use copper, such as cable, transformer and switchgear makers, pay less for it after a lag of about a quarter.
- **Horizon:** weeks (the forced selling) to years (the supply effect).
- **What would invalidate it:** a real collapse in Chinese demand, which would make the fall about fundamentals rather than forced selling.

### 3. The US moves to speed up power-line building
- **Trigger:** the Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act 62-33 on 19 Nov ([Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/nov/19/senate-passes-bipartisan-american-affordability-jobs-act-without/)). It now goes to the House, where the outcome is uncertain.
- **In plain English:** building a US power line can take a decade of paperwork. The bill shortens environmental reviews and lets federal regulators approve lines that states sit on. If it becomes law, more lines get built sooner, and each one needs high-voltage cable, transformers and contractors that are already in short supply. The few companies that can make those, such as Prysmian, Siemens Energy and Quanta, should see fuller order books and firmer prices. More grid capacity could also ease the local power-price spikes that are turning voters against data centres.
- **Second-order chain:** faster permits mean more grid projects. That tightens supply of cable and transformers, which gives suppliers pricing power. The extra grid also uses more copper over the years.
- **Horizon:** months to years.
- **What would invalidate it:** the House stalls or waters down the bill into 2027.

### 4. UK-EU summit: food checks and carbon markets
- **Trigger:** Reuters reports the UK and EU are "set to" agree today to remove certificates and checks on animal and plant products and to link their carbon markets ([Investing.com/Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/britain-and-eu-set-to-agree-on-food-trade-and-youth-mobility-93CH-5040707)). This is a single source and the deal is not yet signed.
- **In plain English:** less paperwork at the border lowers costs for UK food exporters and importers. Linking the carbon markets means UK companies would pay roughly the EU price for carbon permits, which is currently higher. That raises costs for gas-fired power stations and makes wind farms, which pay no carbon cost, relatively more profitable. SSE owns many UK wind farms.
- **Horizon:** months to years.
- **What would invalidate it:** the summit fails, or the deal is watered down.
