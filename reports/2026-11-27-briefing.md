## High-Conviction Trends

### 1. Nvidia's crisis spreads to its credit rating and to sales in South-East Asia
- **Trigger:** S&P CreditWatch negative (25 Nov); FT/Reuters report of the halt to four countries (26 Nov).
- **In plain English:** Washington wants proof that Nvidia's chips are not leaking to China through South-East Asia. So Nvidia has stopped shipping servers there for now, and a credit agency has said its board oversight looks weaker.
  - Chips that cannot go to Malaysia or Singapore will probably go to the US, Europe, Japan and the Middle East instead.
  - That helps data-centre landlords in "licence-safe" countries and the companies selling them power and cooling equipment.
  - It also creates demand for software that checks who buyers are and tracks where each chip ends up.
- **Second-order chain:** export rules -> South-East Asian data-centre projects delayed -> capacity moves to the US and allied countries -> data-centre landlords (Digital Realty, Equinix) and power suppliers (Vertiv, Schneider, already on the watchlist) gain. China's push for home-made chips speeds up.
- **Why no new idea:** every route in adds more US, AI and dollar risk, which your portfolio already over-owns. The data-centre landlords also look expensive (see the table).
- **Horizon:** months.
- **What would invalidate it:** Nvidia says the pause was a short paperwork check and shipments resume within weeks.

### 2. Beijing props up its stock market; copper bounces
- **Trigger:** State Council meeting, 27 Nov. "National team" state funds get a bigger role; margin requirements fall from 100% to 80% from 30 Nov (Reuters/Xinhua via Investing.com).
- **In plain English:** China's government says it will use state money to stop share prices falling, and has made it cheaper to borrow to buy shares. That cheered investors about China, which buys about half the world's copper, so copper and mining shares bounced. Your COPG gains directly.
  - But state buying lifts share prices, not the amount of copper factories actually use.
  - Unless China's factory surveys around 30 Nov improve, the bounce rests on mood.
- **Second-order chain:** state buying -> Chinese shares and copper bounce -> copper miners (COPG) gain. Higher trading volumes help Hong Kong brokers and the exchange, but those are not available on Freetrade. If Beijing also pushes state companies to pay bigger dividends, Chinese state banks and energy companies could be re-valued upwards, as happened in Japan.
- **Opportunity:** HSBC MSCI China ETF (HMCH.L) is a cheap, pound-priced fund at its 52-week low. It is a sentiment trade only (10/15), and it doubles up on the China risk COPG already carries.
- **Horizon:** days to weeks.
- **What would invalidate it:** weak Chinese factory surveys around 30 Nov, or the rally fading within days as past ones did.
