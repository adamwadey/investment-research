## High-Conviction Trends

### 1. Nvidia's political troubles spread into a global chip sell-off - but orders for data-centre kit keep growing
- **Trigger:** Megaspeed blacklisted (Al Jazeera, 24 Nov); AG and Congress questions (BBC, 25 Nov); Micron downgrade (24 Nov); Dell results (SEC 8-K, 24 Nov); Goldman forecasts 52 GW of new US data-centre capacity in 2028 (Reuters via Investing.com, 24 Nov).
- **In plain English:** Investors who rushed back into chip shares on Monday lost their nerve on Tuesday, because the questions about chips reaching China, and about the White House's role in Nvidia's boardroom, have not been answered. The selling spread to every company linked to AI computers. But on the same night Dell said it has $132bn of AI server orders still to deliver. Those servers have to be plugged into power and cooled, so the companies that make that equipment should keep selling, even while their shares are being sold with the chip makers.
- **Second-order chain:** chip panic -> sound equipment suppliers sold alongside -> but Dell's backlog means data centres are still being fitted out -> power-distribution and cooling makers (Vertiv, Legrand, Schneider) keep delivering. Separately, the memory-chip downgrade warns that chip-equipment orders from memory makers (Lam Research, on the watchlist) could slow in 2027.
- **Horizon:** days to weeks for the sell-off; months for the deliveries.
- **What would invalidate it:** a large cloud company cutting its 2027 spending, or proven accounting problems at Nvidia.

### 2. Rates up, dollar up, metals down
- **Trigger:** 96.4% chance of a Fed rate rise on 9 Dec (CME FedWatch); dollar near a 19-month high; copper's lowest close since March; gold near a 15-month low around $3,523/oz (Reuters via Investing.com, 25 Nov). US PCE inflation due 13:30 UK today.
- **In plain English:** US inflation is still well above target, so the Fed is expected to raise interest rates again. Higher US rates make the dollar stronger, and a strong dollar pushes down metals priced in dollars. Mining shares fall even faster than the metal - that is why your COPG is at its low. The same move helps banks, which earn more on the gap between savers' and borrowers' rates, and helps makers of electrical equipment whose copper gets cheaper.
- **Second-order chain:** higher rates -> stronger dollar -> cheaper copper -> lower costs for electrical-equipment makers (Legrand, Vertiv) and better margins for banks (HSBC) -> pain for miners and indebted property companies.
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a soft PCE figure, a weaker dollar, or Chinese stimulus.

### 3. New US tariffs on 16 economies expected soon
- **Trigger:** WSJ report, corroborated by Reuters and AP sources, that the US trade office may publish "excess capacity" tariff findings as early as this week; China would face the highest rates and the EU, Japan and Korea about 15% or below (Investing.com/WSJ, 24 Nov; BBC, 25 Nov). **Not officially announced.**
- **In plain English:** The US is preparing to rebuild the broad import taxes the Supreme Court struck down in February, using a different law. European and Asian exporters would lose; companies that already make things inside the US are protected. That partly explains why Siemens and the DAX fell. Siemens has many US factories, so it should be hurt less than pure exporters.
- **Second-order chain:** tariffs -> higher US prices -> more Fed rate rises -> stronger dollar (Trend 2). Also: China may hit back with limits on rare-earth exports (inference).
- **Horizon:** weeks to months.
- **What would invalidate it:** low final rates, delays, or carve-outs.

### 4. UK energy bills to jump 26% in January
- **Trigger:** Ofgem confirmed this morning that the typical household bill rises from £1,723 to £2,166 a year from 1 Jan, because gas stays expensive while shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is disrupted (BBC; Guardian, 25 Nov).
- **In plain English:** British households will have about £443 a year less to spend on everything else. That hurts pubs, restaurants and mid-market shops, keeps inflation high and makes it harder for the Bank of England to cut rates. In theory UK gas producers and discount grocers gain - but **none of the companies we checked passed today** (see Rejected): Centrica is selling most of its gas fields, Harbour Energy's data look unreliable, and Tesco is already at its analyst target.
- **Horizon:** months.
- **What would invalidate it:** a Hormuz ceasefire that cuts gas prices, or government bill support.

### 5. Big drug companies keep buying growth
- **Trigger:** Sanofi to buy Insmed for $175 a share in cash, about $41bn (Insmed filing, 24 Nov); RBC downgraded Insmed today as its price is now capped.
- **In plain English:** Sanofi's best-selling drug will soon face cheap copies, so it is paying a big premium for a company with a fast-growing lung drug. This supports the value of mid-sized biotechs - but biotech funds already jumped (XBI +7.9% in five days), so buying now would be chasing. It also underlines the value of growth Lilly already has.
- **Horizon:** months.
